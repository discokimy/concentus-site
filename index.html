Ready for band and orchestra

For woodwind and brass players in concert band, marching band and orchestra, and the directors and parents who help them practice.

  • A tuner that reads in your key. Clarinet, trumpet, sax and horn players see the note as written on their part, with concert pitch alongside.
  • Band tuning notes. Play concert B♭ or concert A, shown as the note you'd read.
  • Scales on the staff. Every scale written out in your instrument's key and kept within its range.
  • Learn to read music. A plain-language guide to the grand staff, then note-reading practice from the first few notes to ledger lines and key signatures.

More ways to practice

The tools around the tuner: keep time, hear yourself, and look after what you play.

  • Metronome and drone. Tap a tempo, set subdivisions and accents, and hold a steady drone to tune long notes against.
  • A recorder with up to 4 layers. As simple as a voice memo, with timing calibration for AirPods and other Bluetooth headphones. Recordings stay on your device unless you share them.
  • Practice log and learning paths. Your minutes and streak are counted for you, and step-by-step paths show what to do next.
  • Instrument guide. What your instrument is, what its parts do, where it's from and where it's heard.
  • Restringing and care guides. Plain steps for looking after your instrument, and what to do how often.
  • Real instrument sounds. Recorded sounds for guitars, bass, violin, viola, cello, pianos, organ, harpsichord and most winds and brass, with a synthesized tone on the other instruments until they're recorded.
